---
title: "Why your landing page isn't converting (and what to fix first)"
excerpt: "People visit your page but hardly anyone gets in touch. Here's what to check, in what order, and the simple changes that usually get inquiries moving."
---
You have a page for your business, people are visiting, but the messages aren't coming in. Or only a few trickle through. Before you rebuild everything or spend more on ads, it's worth running an orderly check. Most of the time the problem comes down to a handful of specific things you can review in an afternoon.

Let's go through them one by one, with what to look at and what to change.

## The top of the page doesn't say what you do, who it's for or why you

The first thing visitors see, before scrolling, should answer three questions: what you offer, who it's for and why they should pick you. If people have to guess, they leave.

A hypothetical example for a gym:

- **Before:** "Welcome to Strength Gym, your best self starts today."
- **After:** "Small-group functional training downtown. 50-minute classes with a coach who corrects your form, open 7 am to 10 pm."

The second headline isn't more creative. It's more useful. It says what, where and what sets it apart.

**What to do:** show your page to someone who doesn't know you for five seconds and ask what you sell. If they can't tell you, rewrite the headline.

## Too many options, or no clear next step

Plenty of pages have five different buttons, a long menu, social links and a form, all competing for attention. Visitors don't know what you want them to do.

**What to check:**

- What's the one thing you want people to do? Book, request a quote, send a message.
- Is that button easy to spot, and does it show up again further down the page?
- Is anything pulling attention away, like social media links at the very top?

**What to change:** pick one main action and make it stand out. Other options can stay, just quieter.

## Slow loading, especially on phones with mobile data

A lot of people visit from their phone, on the go, using mobile data. If the page takes too long to appear, they're gone before reading a single line.

The usual suspects:

- Huge images uploaded straight from the camera.
- Plugins or widgets nobody uses that still load on every visit.
- Background videos, sliders and too many fonts.

**How to check:** go to PageSpeed Insights (a free Google tool), paste your page's address and look at the mobile results. Don't obsess over the score; focus on what it flags as the main issues. You can also just try it yourself on your phone with wifi turned off.

**What to change:** compress and resize your images, remove what isn't pulling its weight and review which plugins you actually still need.

## There's nothing that builds trust

Someone who doesn't know you needs reasons to believe you're real and good at what you do. A page full of stock photos and generic copy doesn't give them any.

Picture a small business renting cabins in the mountains. What builds trust:

- Real photos of the cabins, the grounds and the view.
- Guest reviews, ideally with names or linked to Google.
- Who's behind it: a photo and a couple of lines about the owners.
- Location with a map, a phone number and contact hours.

For an accountant the same idea applies in a different form: the kind of clients they work with, years in practice, credentials, and where the office is.

**What to change:** replace everything generic with your own material, even if it's not perfect. A real photo taken on your phone often works better than a flawless stock image.

## It was built for a desktop, but your visitors are on phones

Pages are often designed and approved on a big monitor. Then on a phone the text is tiny, the buttons are cramped and the form is a pain to fill in.

**What to check on your own phone:**

- Can you read it without zooming?
- Can you tap the buttons without missing?
- Does the main button appear quickly, without much scrolling?
- Can people tap your phone number to call right away?

**What to change:** review and fix the mobile version first. For many businesses, the desktop version is the secondary one.

## The ad promises one thing and the page shows another

Say your post reads "full car service, 20% off this month" and the link drops people on the auto shop's homepage, with every service listed and no mention of the deal. Visitors feel misled, or they simply can't find what they came for.

**What to change:** every important ad or campaign should lead to a page (or section) that repeats the same offer, using the same words and the same image. That continuity reassures people.

## Forms that ask for too much

Every extra field is another reason to give up. Asking for a street address, tax ID, how they heard about you and a required message, just for a first inquiry, is a lot.

**What to change:**

- Ask only for what you need to reply: a name and a way to reach them is enough to start.
- You can learn the rest in the conversation.

And if your customers use WhatsApp (very common in Argentina and many other places), a button that opens a chat with a prefilled message helps a lot. For the auto shop it might read: "Hi, I'd like to ask about a service for my car. Make and year:". People just fill in the blank and hit send, and you already know which page they came from.

## You're not measuring anything

Without data, every change is a guess. You don't need anything fancy, just the basics.

**What to set up:**

- An analytics tool (Google Analytics or something similar) to see how many people visit and where they come from.
- Events for the actions that matter: WhatsApp clicks, form submissions, taps on your phone number.

**How to use it:** change one thing at a time and give it a reasonable amount of time before drawing conclusions. If you swap the headline, the button and the photos on the same day, you won't know what made the difference.

## Checklist: where to start

If you're short on time, go in this order:

1. Rewrite the main headline so it says what you do, who it's for and why you.
2. Choose a single main action and make it easy to see.
3. Go through the whole page on your phone, using mobile data.
4. Run it through PageSpeed Insights and shrink any heavy images.
5. Add real photos, reviews and complete contact details.
6. Add a WhatsApp button with a prefilled message and trim your form.
7. Set up basic tracking and test one change at a time.
